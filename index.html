Who it is for · why it matters

One system has to work for the advisor and the bank.

The advisor needs better decisions in the moment. The bank needs commercial strategy to become governed, explainable client-level action.

For the advisor

Know who. Know why now. Know what fits. Know what changes.

Prioritise attention from client events, cash, maturity, allocation and bank signals.

Navigate products in context instead of judging a product in isolation.

See the portfolio consequence before turning an idea into a recommendation.

Explain and act with the rationale and decision state still attached.

For the bank

Turn product strategy into governed advisor action.

Activate campaigns and priority products against relevant client opportunities.

Keep commercial intent explicit without bypassing PIW eligibility or client context.

Make prioritisation explainable through visible client, portfolio and commercial reasoning.

Create a bounded place for AI without handing it authority over financial truth.

A few terms, if wealth-advisory language isn't your day job

PIW
The governed eligibility boundary — which products a given client may be offered at all.
RM / advisor
Relationship Manager: the banker who owns the client relationship. Used interchangeably with "advisor" here.
Reference allocation
A target portfolio mix the client's current holdings are compared against.
Suitability review
The downstream compliance check that a specific recommendation fits the client, before anything can be executed.
OMS
Order Management System: the bank's production system that actually places trades. DecisionThread stops at its boundary.

The problem

The bank does not lack systems. The decision is fragmented across them.

A single recommendation can cross client records, holdings, product teams, research, portfolio analysis, proposal workflows and different execution platforms. Each handoff risks losing the context that made the decision sensible.

01 · AI as a decision-context layer

The meeting becomes a live decision input.

A material client utterance can update meeting context, briefing and product priorities inside the same advisor workflow.

AI can change context. It cannot overwrite financial truth.

PIW, holdings, product facts, reference allocation and deterministic portfolio calculations remain governed by authoritative state and rules.

02 · Governed commercial decisioning

“Eligible” is only the beginning of a product decision.

DecisionThread lets commercial priorities influence advisor attention inside explicit client boundaries.

PIW eligible universe→ Client Relevance+ Portfolio Context+ Commercial Priority→ Advisor prioritisation

03 · Proposal → portfolio consequence

A recommendation is not finished when a product is selected.

Product + amount + account + funding source become a proposed portfolio, so the advisor can see what the decision actually changes.

Decision support preserves trade-offs

A proposal can improve one dimension and worsen another. The goal is not to manufacture a universally positive sales output; it is to make consequences visible before the client acts.

04 · Cross-product truth

One advisor workflow does not require one analytical model.

A unified experience should not erase how different financial products actually behave.

05 · Explain → hand off

The decision thread survives the system boundary.

Proposal identity, product, amount, account and funding source remain attached when the case moves toward downstream suitability and transaction review.

DecisionThread does not pretend to be the bank’s production OMS. It preserves the decision state required to cross that boundary without rebuilding the reasoning from scratch.

Commercial workflow

Opportunity can start from the client — or from the bank.

Product Campaigns reverses the entry point: start from a campaign or priority product, then identify PIW-eligible, contextually relevant clients. Commercial sales enablement stays part of the system architecture rather than being hidden behind neutral language.

Design judgments

The interesting part is the decision system behind the screens.

Commercial intent is explicit.

Bank priorities are legitimate decision inputs, but never a way around client eligibility.

PIW is a boundary, not personalisation.

Current portfolio, events and meeting intent can materially change what deserves attention.

AI augments context, not authority.

Conversational intelligence can reshape the workflow while financial state remains deterministic and governed.

Portfolio impact beats isolated product logic.

The proposal is evaluated by what it does to the client’s whole portfolio, including trade-offs.

Advisor taxonomy can differ from analytical taxonomy.

The interface can match how advisors speak without forcing the analytics to use the same categories.

Continuity matters through execution.

The rationale and proposal state are designed to survive downstream control boundaries.

Prototype scope

Functional proof, not a production-bank claim.

The public build is a functional interactive prototype designed to make the product and decision architecture observable.

  • Synthetic client and product data.
  • Scripted conversational inputs demonstrate the intended AI interaction and authority model.
  • Enterprise integrations and downstream systems are simulated at their control boundaries.
  • Portfolio and product analytics demonstrate decision logic; they are not production suitability or advice engines.
  • The product capabilities and workflow design are the claim. Production deployment, measured commercial uplift and live-bank integrations are not.
